# 3D view display settings — design record

## The need, in the owner's words

"On Soban's 3D view, we need the following options: display measurements (only those readable at current zoom level); display unselected elements (when a branch of the tree is selected), solid or transparent, or not at all. Where would you see them?"

On placement, after discussion: "I'd say it goes just below the tree on desktop, and in a settings modal on phone, small settings button above or over (right hand corner) the view." The owner then accepted a bottom sheet over the strip of sliders instead of a modal on the phone ("ok, even on desktop?"), and, told that on desktop the settings stay visible under the tree, said "ok prototype. Make it right the first time".

## The product

The 3D view is one HTML page generated by furniture-design (`furniture_design/viewer.py`, `PAGE` and `viewer_html`) for each version of a piece; Soban shows it in an iframe (in the sheet a card opens on a phone, in the page's column on a wide screen). It is written in the piece's language (`WORDS`, fr and en).

What it has today (viewer.py lines 24–86, 195–370):

- Wide (over 720 px): the model on the left; on the right a 300 px column, the tree (`#nav`: the title that leads back up, the size of what is shown, the list of assemblies then parts) over the tools (`#tools`: the assembly step slider, drawn only with two steps or more; the pull-apart slider; a row with the Connectors checkbox and Reset).
- Narrow (720 px and under): the tree on top (at most 38 % of the height), the model, the tools at the bottom (at most 30 %).
- Picking an assembly or a part row shows it alone, fitted (`go()`, `under()`): what is outside the branch is not drawn at all.
- A tap on a part shows its letter, name, size and material in a box at the top left of the model (`#tip`); a double tap brings the view close to the point touched.

Ledger entries that govern this screen (furniture-design's ledger): `the-tree-leads-the-3d-views-panel`, `on-a-narrow-screen-the-tree-stands-above-the-model`, `a-part-row-opens-the-part-alone`, `a-profile-is-one-part-with-its-section`, `a-held-part-is-pulled-apart-the-way-its-holder-opens`, `the-documents-are-for-the-people-who-build`.

The data for the prototype is a real piece: the owner's wardrobe doors (Portes des penderies, V7): 36 parts in two wardrobes, each with four rails (an aluminium angle with a U glued in it) and two sliding doors (a PVC frame of four 8 × 8 bars around a 6 mm polycarbonate sheet), assemblies three deep (Grande penderie › Porte avant › Cadre).

## The list: what changes and nothing else

Requested:
1. Dimensions can be shown on the model, only those readable at the current zoom.
2. When a branch is open, what is outside it can be drawn hidden (as today), see-through, or solid.

Agreed placement:
3. Wide: the display settings stand just below the tree, at the head of the tools.
4. Narrow: a small button over the top right corner of the model opens the display settings in a sheet rising from the bottom over the strip of sliders, leaving the model in view.

Side effects, each with the effect it manages:
5. The Connectors checkbox moves with the two new settings (it is the same kind of setting: what is drawn). On a phone this frees the strip for the sliders alone. Reset, which resets the sliders and the branch, stays with the sliders on its own row.
6. Connectors becomes a `<label>` around its checkbox, as the two new rows are: today its text is a `<span>` and only the 13 px box toggles it (a target under 24 px, TARGETS-ARE-THUMB-SIZED).

Nothing else on the page changes.

## The elements

### E1. The display settings block

Three rows, in this order, the same on every width:

- `☑ Connecteurs` (en: Connectors), checked by default as today.
- `☐ Cotes` (en: Dimensions), unchecked by default. "Cotes" and "Dimensions" are the words the plans already use ("Cotes en mm", "Dimensions in mm").
- `Reste du meuble` (en: Rest of the piece) over a segmented control of three: `Masqué · Transparent · Plein` (en: Hidden · See-through · Solid), Masqué selected by default, so nothing changes for whoever never touches it. This row is drawn only while a branch is open (the same rule as the assembly step slider, which is drawn only when it has two steps); at the piece itself there is no rest.

Each row is at least 44 px tall and the whole row is the target. Settings are not remembered across page loads, and Reset does not change them (it never touched Connectors).

### E2. Wide: where the block stands

At the head of `#tools`, directly under the tree, before the assembly step slider. Below the sliders, Reset on its own row, aligned right as today.

### E3. Narrow: the button

A 44 × 44 px round button over the model, 12 px from its top and right edges, drawn in the panel's colour with the line colour as border (as `#tip` is). Its icon is a stack of layers (the Material Symbols "layers" glyph), its accessible name "Affichage" (en: Display). The tip box opens at the top left and is at most 70 % wide, so the two do not meet.

### E4. Narrow: the sheet

The button opens the block in a sheet rising from the bottom of the page, over the strip of sliders, as tall as its rows (the three rows fit in the strip's height on a 390 × 844 phone). It is not modal: the model above stays visible and can still be turned, pinched and tapped, and each setting takes effect as it is changed. A close button (×, accessible name "Fermer" / "Close") stands at the sheet's top right; the layers button toggles it too, and Escape closes it. Closing it shows the strip of sliders again. The button shows its pressed state (`aria-expanded`) while the sheet is open.

### E5. Dimensions on the model

What is measured is what is shown (the branch, or the whole piece), never the rest, and only the parts drawn at the current assembly step. Along each of the three axes, as the plans do it (`sheets._view_dims`):

- the overall size of what is shown, and
- the chain of distinct part edges along that axis: the distance between each pair of neighbouring edge positions.

Each axis is dimensioned along one edge of the box of what is shown: of the four box edges parallel to that axis, the one on the box's outline that is nearest the bottom of the screen (for the two horizontal axes) or the leftmost (for the vertical axis), offset outward from the box by a fixed gap on screen, the chain nearer the box and the overall size beyond it, with extension lines back to the box. The dimension lines are drawn in 3D projected to the screen every frame; the numbers are always upright, set in the page's font at 12 px in the ink colour on a panel-coloured background, in mm without unit, formatted as the list writes sizes (one decimal at most).

Readable means: a dimension is drawn only when its line on screen is longer than its number's width plus 16 px, and its number does not overlap a number already drawn. Numbers are placed overall first, then the chain from the longest down, so zooming in brings the short ones in and zooming out drops them.

While the parts are pulled apart (the slider above 0), no dimension is drawn: no part is where its dimension would say it is.

### E6. The rest of the piece

While a branch is open:
- Masqué: as today, only the branch is drawn.
- Transparent: the parts outside the branch are drawn at 15 % opacity with their edges at 25 %, writing no depth, so the branch shows through them.
- Plein: the parts outside the branch are drawn opaque, in a light grey instead of the wood colour, so the branch stays the one thing in wood.

In both, the rest is drawn finished and in its place: the assembly steps and pulling apart act on the branch only, the connectors drawn are the branch's only, the view stays fitted to the branch, and a tap never picks a part of the rest (taps pass through it).

## Alternatives

The placement is agreed; what the dimensions show is the open question:

- **A. The plans' chains (E5 as written).** Positions along the box of what is shown: where each part starts and ends, as the plans dimension a view.
- **B. Each part's own size.** For each part shown, its length, width and thickness along its own box edges (the edge nearest the bottom of the screen for each), under the same readability rule, longest first. No chains, no overall size.
