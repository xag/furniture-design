# 3D view display settings — design record

## The need, in the owner's words

"On Soban's 3D view, we need the following options: display measurements (only those readable at current zoom level); display unselected elements (when a branch of the tree is selected), solid or transparent, or not at all. Where would you see them?"

On placement, after discussion: "I'd say it goes just below the tree on desktop, and in a settings modal on phone, small settings button above or over (right hand corner) the view." The owner then accepted a bottom sheet over the strip of sliders instead of a modal on the phone ("ok, even on desktop?"), and, told that on desktop the settings stay visible under the tree, said "ok prototype. Make it right the first time".

On what the dimensions show, shown A (positions) and B (each part's size) as prototyped: "I'm sorry but B is unreadable as prototyped"; told that A gives where parts are and B each part's own size: "So we need both options".

## The product

The 3D view is one HTML page generated by furniture-design (`furniture_design/viewer.py`, `PAGE` and `viewer_html`) for each version of a piece; Soban shows it in an iframe (in the sheet a card opens on a phone, in the page's column on a wide screen). It is written in the piece's language (`WORDS`, fr and en).

What it has today (viewer.py on main):

- Wide (over 720 px): the model on the left; on the right a 300 px column, the tree (`#nav`: the title that leads back up, the size of what is shown, the list of assemblies then parts) over the tools (`#tools`: the assembly step slider, drawn only with two steps or more; the pull-apart slider; a row with the Connectors checkbox and Reset).
- Narrow (720 px and under): the tree on top (at most 38 % of the height), the model, the tools at the bottom (at most 30 %).
- Picking an assembly or a part row shows it alone, fitted (`go()`, `under()`): what is outside the branch is not drawn at all.
- A tap on a part shows its letter, name, size and material in a box at the top left of the model (`#tip`); a double tap brings the view close to the point touched.

Ledger entries that govern this screen (furniture-design's ledger): `the-tree-leads-the-3d-views-panel`, `on-a-narrow-screen-the-tree-stands-above-the-model`, `a-part-row-opens-the-part-alone`, `a-profile-is-one-part-with-its-section`, `a-held-part-is-pulled-apart-the-way-its-holder-opens`, `the-documents-are-for-the-people-who-build`.

The data for the prototype is a real piece: the owner's wardrobe doors (Portes des penderies, V7): 36 parts in two wardrobes, each with four rails (an aluminium angle with a U glued in it) and two sliding doors (a PVC frame of four 8 × 8 bars around a 6 mm polycarbonate sheet), assemblies three deep (Grande penderie › Porte avant › Cadre). `mock/piece.json` is the store's V3 piece.json with V7's steps, version and instructions; its parts, sizes and positions are V7's.

## The list: what changes and nothing else

Requested:
1. Dimensions can be shown on the model, only those readable at the current zoom: positions or sizes, either one.
2. When a branch is open, what is outside it can be drawn hidden (as today), transparent, or solid.

Agreed placement:
3. Wide: the display settings stand just below the tree, at the head of the tools.
4. Narrow: the display settings open at the bottom, in the strip of sliders, from a Display button in its bottom row beside Reset, leaving the model in view (the owner first proposed a settings button over the model's top right corner, then turned it down once drawn).

Side effects, each with the effect it manages:
5. The Connectors checkbox moves with the two new settings (it is the same kind of setting: what is drawn). On a phone this frees the strip for the sliders alone. Reset, which resets the sliders and the branch, stays with the sliders on its own row.
6. Connectors becomes a `<label>` around its checkbox, as the two new rows are: today its text is a `<span>` and only the 13 px box toggles it (a target under 24 px, TARGETS-ARE-THUMB-SIZED; axe reports the box as unlabelled).
7. A line between the tree and the tools on a wide screen, as the narrow layout already draws: with the settings directly under the parts list, "Connecteurs" read as one more row of the list.

Nothing else on the page changes.

## The elements

### E1. The display settings block

Three rows, in this order, the same on every width:

- `☑ Connecteurs` (en: Connectors), checked by default as today.
- `Cotes` (en: Dimensions) over a segmented control of three radio buttons: `Masquées · Positions · Tailles` (en: Hidden · Positions · Sizes), Masquées selected by default, so nothing changes for whoever never touches it. "Cotes" and "Dimensions" are the words the plans already use ("Cotes en mm", "Dimensions in mm"); Positions and Tailles are drafting's location and size dimensions (E5). One choice of three rather than two checkboxes: both kinds at once would bring back the clutter that made B unreadable.
- `Reste du meuble` (en: Rest of the piece) over a segmented control of three radio buttons: `Masqué · Transparent · Plein` (en: Hidden · Transparent · Solid), Masqué selected by default, so nothing changes for whoever never touches it. This row is drawn only while a branch is open (the same rule as the assembly step slider, which is drawn only when it has two steps); at the piece itself there is no rest. Opening or leaving a branch moves the sliders under the block by this row's height, as the assembly block already moves them.

Connecteurs stays a checkbox, as it is today and as model-viewer's "Show Dimensions" is. Its row is a `<label>` in the ink colour (not the muted caption style of the slider labels), its box 18 px at the left, the whole row the target. The two choices of three have the same look, one under the other. Rows are 44 px tall where any pointer is a finger, and 32 px where none is (`not (any-pointer: coarse)`), the size of the buttons beside them, over WCAG's 24 px floor, so the block fits under the tree on more laptops. Settings are not remembered across page loads, and Reset does not change them (it never touched Connectors).

### E2. Where the block stands

With room for it: at the head of `#tools`, directly under the tree, before the assembly step slider. Below the sliders, Reset on its own row, aligned right as today.

There is room for it when the screen is wider than 720 px and the tools, with the block in them, fit their place in the column without scrolling (checked whenever the page is resized or a branch opened). Otherwise (a phone; a phone held sideways; a low laptop window; a piece whose parts list fills the column) the block is behind the Display button (E3, E4). The layout of the rest of the page still follows the width alone, as today.

### E3. The Display button

Where the block is not shown under the tree: the bottom row of the strip under the model holds a text button, "Affichage" (en: Display) with a chevron, at the left, and Reset at the right as today. Nothing is drawn over the model. The chevron points down while the block is closed and up while it is open (stage two: the blind judge read an upward chevron as "expanded"; NN/g and Material draw a closed disclosure with a downward one), and the button carries `aria-expanded` (the W3C disclosure pattern). It has Reset's size (32 px tall), the button beside it, over WCAG's 24 px floor.

The owner, on the first version, which had a round layers button over the model's top right corner: "I find the button ugly and a friction. I thought you wouldn't keep it when you talked about a menu attached to the bottom". A text label also settles round 1's F6 (an icon alone is ambiguous).

### E4. The block opened

The Display button puts the block in the sliders' place, in the strip, above the bottom row: the sliders are hidden while it is open, and the strip grows to the block's height, so the model's area takes what is left: smaller where the block is taller than the sliders (in a branch), larger where it is shorter (at the piece, where the assembly slider and its sentence go); the model stays wholly in view, and the dimensions drawn follow the new scale. It never lies over the model, so the dimensions along its bottom stay visible. Open, the block takes the height it needs from the tree, never from the model, down to the tree's title and size line (`give()`), so the model keeps its scale and a change is seen as it is; on the smallest screens the strip may still scroll, and the bottom row stays pinned to the strip's foot while the block is open, so the button that closes it is always in reach. Each setting takes effect as it is changed, and the model can still be turned, pinched and tapped.

It closes by the Display button again, or Escape. Closing it brings the sliders back. It adds no step to the browser's history, so the phone's Back closes the whole 3D view as it does today (`mock/backtest.mjs` checks one Back closes the view in a parent page that, like Soban's sheet, closes on Back).

### E5. Dimensions on the model

What is measured is what is shown (the branch, or the whole piece), never the rest, and only the parts drawn at the current assembly step. Two kinds, one at a time:

- **Positions**, along each of the three axes, as the plans do it (`sheets._view_dims`): the overall size of what is shown, and the chain of distinct part edges along that axis, the distance between each pair of neighbouring edge positions.
- **Tailles**: the size, along each axis, of each row of the list at the level shown, as the list gives it: an assembly as the box of its parts, a part once however many times it is used. Two rows with the same span along an axis (the front and back rails) give one size.

Each axis is dimensioned along one edge of the box of what is shown: of the four box edges parallel to that axis, the one on the box's outline that is nearest the bottom of the screen (for the two horizontal axes) or the leftmost (for the vertical axis), offset outward from the box on screen in rows: the first 18 px off, the next ones as far apart as a number is deep across its line (22 px for a horizontal line, the widest number's width plus 14 px for a vertical one), so a row's numbers clear the next. Positions puts its chain in the first row and the overall size in the second. Tailles puts the shortest size nearest, each in the first row where it overlaps no other size on that side, as a drawing stacks its dimensions; a size that is then not drawn gives up its row. A Tailles size stands on the opposite edge (the highest, or the rightmost for the vertical axis) when its row is nearer that edge by a quarter of the box, as an elevation dimensions a top rail above the view; a row as tall or as deep as the box stays on the near edge. A dimension stays on the edge it was on until another edge is lower (or more to the left) by 12 px, so turning the model a little never moves a number; the edge is kept across the assembly steps. Each end has an extension line from the corner of the part it is measured from (of the parts with an edge at that position, the corner nearest the dimensioned edge) to just past the dimension line, so a number names the parts it is between even in a turned view. The lines and numbers are drawn over the model, never hidden by it; the numbers are always upright, 12 px in the ink colour on a panel-coloured ground, in mm without unit, as the list writes sizes (one decimal at most).

Readable means: a dimension is drawn only when its line on screen is longer than its number's width plus 16 px, its number is wholly on screen, its number does not overlap a number already drawn, none of its lines passes through a number already drawn, and no line already drawn passes through its number. Numbers are placed overall first, then the chain from the longest down, in mm, so the order never changes as the view turns and a longer neighbour always takes the place before a shorter one.

While the parts are pulled apart (the slider above 0), no dimension is drawn: no part is where its dimension would say it is.

### E6. The rest of the piece

While a branch is open:
- Masqué: as today, only the branch is drawn.
- Transparent: the parts outside the branch are drawn in grey `#8f8b85` at 15 % opacity with their outlines at 25 %, writing no depth, so the branch shows through them; a tap passes through them to the branch.
- Plein: the parts outside the branch are drawn opaque in the same grey, without their outlines, so the branch is told apart by its outlines as well as its colour (WCAG 1.4.1: not by colour alone); a tap on them selects nothing and does not pass to a part they hide; a double tap on them brings the view close to the point touched, as a double tap anywhere on the model does.

In both, the rest is drawn finished and in its place, and not at all while the parts are pulled apart (they would pass into it); the assembly steps and pulling apart act on the branch only, the connectors drawn are the branch's only, and the view stays fitted to the branch.

## Alternatives

The placement is agreed. What the dimensions show was put as A (the plans' chains) or B (each part's own size, on its own edges). B as prototyped drew every part's three sizes across the door faces, and the owner found it "unreadable as prototyped"; told that the two answer different questions, the owner said "So we need both options". Both are now in, as Cotes : Positions · Tailles (E1, E5), Tailles drawn off the outline in rows instead of on the parts.

## Stage two (one pass, two testers, cheapest model)

- The blind judge was given the sheet `stills/judge/J-390-light.png` (eight states of A at 390 px, light) and the skill's one-sentence prompt, nothing else; it saw no earlier verdict.
